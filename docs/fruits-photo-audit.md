# Fruit photograph audit — 21 September 2026

Reviewed every one of the 125 fruit photographs in before/after contact sheets, with direct enlargement checks for suspect and replacement images. Replaced 36 weak photographs and refreshed the remaining 89 from their credited originals. The audit prioritized recognizable fruit, visible skin or interior texture, clear focus, useful framing, correct identity, and reusable photographic sources.

## Findings and changes

- Replaced distant, foliage-heavy or low-resolution pictures with close fruit views. Examples include banana, pineapple, blueberry, blackcurrant, Cape gooseberry, olive, persimmon and greengage.
- Replaced cupuaçu's prepared-dessert image with a freshly opened fruit showing its fibrous pulp and shell.
- Preferred cut and whole fruit together when a suitable photograph was available: ambarella, amla, avocado, bael, guava, lychee, persimmon, pulasan and wax apple. Preserved strong existing examples such as mangosteen, rambutan, coconut and finger lime.
- Rejected candidate images that were botanical drawings, cooked dishes, mouldy fruit, unrelated species, badly focused or dominated by the surroundings. Full-size review replaced an initial soft cloudberry and safou selection, and improved greengage and sour cherry again.
- Checked source identity as well as appearance, including Davidsonia jerseyana for Davidson's plum and Malpighia emarginata for acerola. Market groups remain market groups; the photo does not establish a universal color, cultivar or ripeness.
- All files are JPEG photographs, resized without enlargement to at most 1600 pixels on the long edge. 110 reach 1600 pixels; the others retain their original resolution (minimum long edge 975 pixels, minimum short edge 640 pixels). Resolution alone is not a sharpness guarantee, so visual inspection remains necessary.
- The fruit assets total 28.5 MB, compared with 12.7 MB before this audit. They remain local and available through the offline manifest. Collection thumbnails use responsive Next image sizes and lazy loading; the larger view loads on demand.
- All 125 SHA-256 hashes are distinct. A 64-bit difference-hash comparison found no pairs within a Hamming distance of 8. Similar-looking citrus still use different source photographs.
- Photo filenames include a content hash. Existing installed apps and the image optimizer therefore request the refreshed assets instead of reusing responses cached under the old filenames. Card IDs and saved unlocks stay the same.

## Collection presentation

Fruit cards use square photo frames and a responsive minimum column width of 260 pixels, with one column on phones and more columns as space allows. The whole photograph is contained inside the frame. Rarity badges appear below the photograph so they cannot cover fruit detail.

Tapping an unlocked fruit photograph opens a large view with a two-times zoom toggle and a scrollable viewport. Close, Escape, focus trapping/return, and visible photo credit are supported. An image failure has an explicit recovery message; closing and reopening retries the view. Locked cards retain their existing behavior.

The 125 fruit identities, facts, ratings, sourced weights and collection ordering are unchanged. Other categories retain their existing Collection layout.

## Regression coverage

`tests/e2e/fruits-content.spec.ts` checks every asset's dimensions, unique hash, versioned filename and attribution. The tablet/mobile flow verifies uncropped square frames, enlargement, image loading, zoom and scrolling, credit visibility, Escape, keyboard focus return, failure recovery and viewport bounds, alongside the existing gameplay and collection-order checks. Run the consolidated `npm run verify` gate before release.

After changing a fruit image, run `node scripts/fingerprint-fruit-images.mjs`, then `node scripts/generate-fruits-pack.mjs` and `npm run generate:offline-manifest`. The fingerprint command updates only local photo filenames and their source-data paths; it does not download images or change factual content.

Release validation: `npm run qa:fast`, `npm run build` and `npm run test:e2e:run` passed on the final versioned assets (131 logic, 68 desktop and 33 mobile tests). The 1024 × 768 tablet and 390 × 844 phone screenshots were inspected for photo framing, zoom, legible controls and viewport fit. The mobile image check inspects decoded image pixels because the browser reports density-corrected `naturalWidth` values on high-DPI screens.
