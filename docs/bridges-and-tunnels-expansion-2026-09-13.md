# Bridges & Tunnels expansion — 13 September 2026

Added 25 internationally known landmarks: 18 bridges and 7 tunnels. The pack now has 87 cards: the previous 62 plus these additions. The existing 38 US crossings and their state metadata are unchanged.

The additions cover 17 countries across Europe, Asia, Africa, and North America. The selection emphasizes recognizable landmarks, historic construction, unusual shapes, and distinctive tunnel engineering. Fame and Engineering remain editorial game ratings.

Each new source record has a dated factual reference, measurement and opening notes, a representative map location, and the exact photo filename, Commons file page, credited creator, and reuse license. All 25 photographs are local. The source generator uses each new record’s audit date while retaining the previous records’ original audit date.

## Measurement and date decisions

- Pont du Gard uses its surviving 275 m upper tier. The year 50 CE is approximate, within a 40–60 CE construction period; it is not a known opening day.
- Stari Most uses 2004 for the reconstructed bridge, with the original 1566 bridge explained in its fact and opening note.
- Chapel Bridge uses an approximate original date of 1365 and explains its reconstruction after the 1993 fire. The Chain Bridge similarly distinguishes its 1849 first opening from the 1949 rebuilding.
- The Bridge of Sighs is approximately 11 m long, which rounds to 0.01 mi. Its early-1600s construction date is represented approximately as 1600.
- Puente Nuevo uses the tourism office’s first opening to horses and carriages in 1787, with completion in 1793 recorded separately.
- The 25 de Abril figure covers the approximately 2.3 km suspension bridge, excluding the approach viaduct. Vasco da Gama includes its full 17.2 km crossing and approaches.
- The 1915 Çanakkale Bridge is 4,608 m including approach viaducts; 2,023 m is its main span, and 2022 is its opening year.
- Marmaray is explicitly the 1,387 m immersed Bosphorus section, not the 13.6 km initial project or the entire suburban railway.
- SMART uses the 9.7 km stormwater tunnel; its shorter motorway route is identified separately. Its pin follows the mapped tunnel near the Bukit Bintang junction, rather than the coarse city-level coordinate in the initial reference.
- Simplon uses one 19.803 km bore, not the sum of the two parallel bores.

## Image review

The 25 photographs were reviewed together in a contact sheet and in the standard landscape card crop. Stari Most, Glenfinnan, and Puente Nuevo use views that make their arches clear. SMART shows its road interior; Thames and Marmaray show their rail tunnels. Simplon uses a modern photograph of its southern portals rather than the historical construction image returned by the initial search. All previous card photos are preserved.

```sh
npm run verify
```
