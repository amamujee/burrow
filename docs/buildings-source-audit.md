# Buildings factual source audit

Audited 2026-09-05. This follow-up replaces all 51 image-provenance fallbacks and upgrades the nine earlier citations. All 60 building IDs, names, photos, image credits, recognition bands, and rarity behavior are preserved. No assets or game modes were added.

All 60 cards now have factual source entries: 54 have an individually opened CTBUH/CVU Skyscraper Center profile, with architect/operator references where their published measurements differ. There are 64 source entries in total. The three explicitly labeled secondary-source exceptions are Rise Tower (construction trade press), 385 Atlantic Avenue (StreetEasy property listing), and 1555 Blanford Circle (Redfin public-record details).

## Measurement policy

CVU architectural height excludes antennas, while its above-ground floor count describes physical floors, including major mechanical levels. Marketed floor labels can skip numbers and are not interchangeable with this count. The catalog now follows the cited physical count wherever a CVU profile is used. References were read for the exact subject: the old Dubai Creek Tower link accidentally identified a completed 117 m office tower, and the first New York Times search result identified its older 18-floor headquarters.

The Empire State Building keeps its familiar 1,454 ft tip height with an explicit antenna label and a note giving its 1,250 ft architectural height. Eiffel Tower height similarly includes antennas; its three levels are visitor levels. Brooklyn Tower retains the architect’s 1,066 ft figure, while its note discloses CVU’s 1,035 ft figure. 520 Fifth Avenue retains KPF’s 1,002 ft, discloses CVU’s 1,000 ft, and is marked under construction because the cited sources establish phased occupancy and topping-out, not confirmed final completion. The Everly is now finished according to its 2025 completion record.

Rise Tower and the redesigned Dubai Creek Tower retain unknown numerical heights and no fabricated floor count. Dubai Creek is classified as a proposed redesign with the earlier project explicitly on hold. Both local-home heights remain clearly labeled educational estimates; property sources substantiate their story counts, not surveyed heights. These estimates must not be described as independently measured facts.
