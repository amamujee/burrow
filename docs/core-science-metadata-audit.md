# Core science and landmark metadata audit

Audited 2026-09-05. Scope: the 60 buildings, 50 sharks and prehistoric guests, 50 space cards, and 50 jets in `src/lib/game-data.ts`, plus their quiz, collection, Sort, Numbers, and Top Trumps consumers. All 210 card IDs and existing rarity assignments are retained. These categories already have enough breadth; no cards or new game modes were added.

## Coverage and limits

Every record received a structural review of identity, units, image/source references, taxonomy or category, recognition, and measurement caveats. The shark review used species-level Florida Museum profiles for 43 records and specific museum, NOAA, university, or FishBase references for the others. Planet dimensions and moon counts were checked against NASA/JPL. The companion [building source audit](buildings-source-audit.md) documents factual references for all 60 buildings, including 54 opened Council on Vertical Urbanism profiles.

All 50 jets now have actual subject-level factual references, totaling 71 source entries. None rely on a Commons image page as a performance citation. Forty former image-only entries were replaced after checking subject specification pages. Forty-three jets have at least one operator, manufacturer, museum, or aviation-agency reference; 22 also or instead use clearly labeled secondary sources, including direct aviation reporting and Wikipedia specification compilations. Seven have secondary-only references: J-20, Tu-160, L-15, J-10, J-11, J-16, and FC-31. A primary subject reference may establish identity or role without verifying the numerical performance; each accuracy note states the actual performance basis. The Su-57 export brochure and Su-35/Su-34 manufacturer pages were available as indexed text but did not open reliably; their retained domestic performance comes from explicitly secondary specification tables, not a claim of independently verified manufacturer limits.

The jet pass preserves all 50 collection IDs and images. There are 36 known reference speeds, 37 known reference ranges, and 32 aircraft with both values suitable for the existing numerical pool. Mach-only claims without atmospheric conditions, unresolved family/variant figures, and lower bounds without an exact total range remain unknown. Those cards remain available in recognition play and the collection. Published performance remains a reference for a specified variant/configuration, not a promise of the same speed or range for every operator, payload, altitude, or fuel load. Older stellar estimates and some aircraft weight/ceiling references still warrant deeper individual research before claiming comprehensive factual certification. Automated checks establish structure and gameplay safety, not independent truth of every source.

## Material corrections

- Shark speeds: removed 49 unsupported exact swimming speeds. The one retained figure is the shortfin mako's approximate 45 mph burst reported by [NOAA](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/feature-story/new-management-measures-mitigate-shortfin-mako-overfishing). Speed questions require comparable measured values, so the former invented speed comparisons are absent. Power remains an explicitly editorial game rating. Removed the fabricated shark-weight formula entirely.
- Shark identity: each record now has a scientific identity and a scientific family/group. Japanese Sawshark specifies the species represented. The cookiecutter is in Dalatiidae; Dunkleosteus is an armored placoderm; Stethacanthus is a prehistoric chimaera relative. [NHM's evolutionary account](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/shark-evolution-a-450-million-year-timeline.html) supports the latter distinction. The unsupported Stethacanthus length is unknown. Bull and Zambezi retain separate collection IDs but explicitly describe the same species; alias cards are excluded from competing quiz answers.
- Shark size: corrected reference lengths for zebra, bull/Zambezi, spinner, dusky, Galapagos, grey reef, smooth hammerhead, pyjama, bamboo, swell, and Japanese sawsharks against their subject references. Whale shark's 62 ft value is a rounded reported 18.8 m specimen, with uncertainty explained by [NHM](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/whale-sharks-worlds-biggest-shark.html). Megalodon uses the [Smithsonian's roughly 60 ft estimate](https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/sharks-rays/megalodon), with newer larger reconstructions acknowledged; Dunkleosteus follows the smaller [2023 Case Western reconstruction](https://case.edu/news/chunky-dunk-clevelands-prehistoric-sea-monster-may-have-been-shorter-stouter-once-believed), not a claim that its body length is settled.
- Buildings: Rise Tower and Dubai Creek Tower no longer have unconfirmed exact heights in numerical play. Jeddah Tower's planned minimum is explicitly planned; it is not a completed-height record. Removed invented completion-year fallbacks and the arbitrary skyline-fame statistic. Big Ben's fact distinguishes the bell from Elizabeth Tower, consistent with [Parliament](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/building/palace/big-ben/facts-figures/). Eiffel height explicitly includes the antenna, matching [the tower operator](https://www.toureiffel.paris/en/the-monument/key-figures).
- Space: planet diameters consistently use volumetric mean radius, converted to miles; Earth is 7,918 mi. The basis is [JPL planetary physical parameters](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/phys_par.html). Giant-planet moon counts are dated: Jupiter 115, Saturn 293, Uranus 29, Neptune 16, checked against NASA and [JPL's satellite catalog](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/discovery.html). Zero remains correct for Mercury and Venus. The Oort Cloud distance is labeled a representative point, not its inner edge. Neptune's description acknowledges enhanced older imagery.
- Space grading: Sort now compares diameters in miles and temperatures in kelvin. A star's raw solar-radius number can no longer be sorted below a planet's diameter in miles. Distance rounds keep their reference origin consistent (Earth for stars, Sun for solar-system objects). Missing concept measurements display the object type instead of zero. Top Trumps likewise excludes unavailable measurements and keeps real negative Fahrenheit temperatures through a common-unit conversion.
- Jets: corrected both factual references and performance basis across the entire 50-card catalog. Examples include the [F-5E](https://pimaair.org/museum-aircraft/northrop-f-5e/) at 1,080 mph / 1,590 mi and [F-4E](https://pimaair.org/museum-aircraft/mcdonnell-douglas-f-4e/) at 1,485 mph / 1,885 mi using their museum reference variants; [Aero's L-39C](https://www.aero.cz/l-39-albatros/) at 466 mph / 621 mi after converting the manufacturer's 750 km/h and 1,000 km figures; and [Taiwan's IDF](https://air.mnd.gov.tw/EN/Weapon/Weapon_Detail.aspx?CID=221&ID=167) maximum range of 1,020 nautical miles, or 1,174 statute miles. Hornet and Super Hornet now use the [Navy's ferry ranges](https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2383479/fa-18a-d-hornet-and-fa-18ef-super-hornet-strike-fighter/) with explicit tanks/load notes, rather than presenting combat-range values as generic flight ranges.
- Jet uncertainty: B-21, FC-31, J-20, the unsuffixed L-15, the photographed J-10B, and Tejas do not retain unverified exact speed/range pairs. F-117 maximum cruise is not mislabeled as maximum speed; U-2/F-16/SR-71 lower-bound ranges are not treated as exact totals. Mirage 2000's former 960 mi was a conversion of a mission-specific combat radius; it remains unknown for total-range comparisons. The T-50 uses its training variant's 23,638 lb maximum takeoff weight instead of the FA-50 derivative's figure. Obvious ceiling and weight mismatches were corrected where the opened references supplied the right basis; loaded weight is not silently relabeled maximum takeoff weight. Missing measurements receive no fabricated defaults. Firepower remains visibly labeled as an editorial game rating.


## Image correction and footprint

Stephenson 2-18 previously displayed Antares. Its local asset now uses the [2MASS survey view of Stephenson 2-18](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Stephenson_2-18_zoomed_in,_2MASS_survey,_2003.png), credited to 2MASS / Faren29 under CC BY-SA 3.0. The survey source was visually inspected, resized to 700 pixels and converted to a 222 KB JPEG; the card explains that this is a survey view, not a resolved stellar surface. No other images were replaced or generated. The T-50 note identifies its existing FA-50 derivative photograph honestly.

## Verification

`tests/e2e/core-science-metadata.spec.ts` passes five focused logic tests. They cover all 210 collection IDs, scientific identities and alias behavior, unknown measurements in collection and numerical play, the six reported Easy-pool leakage cases, real zero moon counts, dated counts, and common-unit grading over 300 generated space Sort rounds, factual jet-source coverage, corrected reference specifications, and 150 additional jet Sort rounds with unknown figures excluded. The core files pass TypeScript and targeted ESLint. Repository-wide verification and release are handled by the coordinating task.
